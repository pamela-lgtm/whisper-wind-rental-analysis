Section One

What I am working with

I am going to be straight with you about the limits of this analysis, because I would rather you trust the reasoning than the precision.

I searched every unfurnished long-term rental in the corridor with a private pool at this size. Four properties in total. Only two of them have actually leased.

Two leases is not a market. It is a sample. I can tell you what those two homes achieved and adjust honestly from them, but a home like yours does not have a reliable comparable, because very few exist and very few people rent them.

That cuts both ways. There is little direct competition. There are also very few renters looking for a 5,820 square foot home at this price point in Osceola County, and the ones who exist are not shopping every week.

What this table is really telling us

Location is the dominant variable, by a very wide margin. The same quality of home earns $2.00 a square foot in Laureate Park and $1.25 in Isle of Pines. That is not a small adjustment — it is the single largest factor in this analysis.

The one home outside Lake Nona proper is struggling. Eagle Creek at $6,500 has sat 72 days without leasing — and Eagle Creek is a gated golf community with stronger name recognition than Lake Ajay Village.

The only fast lease in the set is in Laureate Park. 9646 Reymont leased in seven days at $2.00 a square foot. The other home that leased, in Isle of Pines, took 39 days at $1.25. That gap — in both rent and speed — is the clearest evidence available of where demand actually sits in this corridor.

The second Laureate Park home is only three days on market, which tells us nothing yet. I have included it for context rather than as evidence.

Section Three

Where the value actually is

I want to be clear about what I am pricing, because it is not what the sale listing emphasizes.

What carries real rental value

  • The home itself. Six bedrooms, 2024 construction, elevator, theater, wine cellar, chef's kitchen, first-floor primary suite. This is the strongest interior package in the comparable set by some distance.
  • The pool and spa. A 20 by 40 screened pool is close to essential at this price point — every comparable that has leased in this segment has one.
  • The land and the outlook. At 0.89 acres you have more than twice the lot of the Laureate Park comparables, with an open view across the water from the rear of the home.
  • The four-car garage with golf cart parking, against two and three bays elsewhere.

The lake — real value, but a limited one

Your home is on the water and the view is genuinely lovely. I have given it value. I have not given it the premium a lakefront listing usually carries, and I want to explain why rather than leave you to discover it through a slow campaign.

There is a difference between a lake you look at and a lake you use. The property has no sandy beach and no private dock.

A renter paying at this level who is specifically seeking a lake lifestyle wants to step off their own property onto their own dock. That renter will keep looking. The renter who takes this home is paying for the house, the pool and the land, and treating the view as a pleasant addition rather than the reason they signed.

So the view carries value and I have accounted for it. It simply carries less than frontage with a dock would, and pricing as though it were the latter is how a home at this level sits for four months.

The address

St. Cloud and an Osceola County address rent for meaningfully less than Lake Nona and Orange County, even for an identical home. That is simply what the data shows, and the two slowest listings in this set are the two outside Lake Nona proper.

It is not a reflection of your home. It is a reflection of where corporate relocations, Medical City professionals and the renters at this price point are looking.

Section Five

Why $5,950 — and what to expect

It goes below the listing that is already stuck

Mobberley at $6,500 has demonstrated that this price does not move a large pool home outside Lake Nona right now. At $5,950 you are $550 beneath it, with 1,000 more square feet, newer construction and a larger lot.

It is $1.02 per square foot

Against $1.60 for Laureate Park, $1.35 for Eagle Creek and $1.25 for Isle of Pines. Large homes always command less per foot, and at $1.02 your home is positioned as the clear value in the segment rather than the most expensive thing in it.

I expect this to take time regardless

I want to set this expectation honestly at the outset. The two homes in this set that leased took 7 and 39 days — both in Lake Nona. The one outside it has taken 72 days and counting.

Even priced correctly, a home of this size and price in Osceola County should be expected to take 60 to 90 days, and I would rather tell you that now than have it come as a disappointment in week six.

Section Six

What the number on the lease actually earns you

This matters more at this price point than at any other, because the daily cost of vacancy is so large.

Every vacant day at $5,950 costs $196. At $6,500 it costs $214. The rent that matters is not the figure on the lease — it is what you collect across twelve months after vacancy, divided by twelve.

Section Seven

This does not meet your expectation

I want to say this plainly rather than let you reach the end of the report and feel I had avoided it.

You told me you expect a high return, and I understood exactly what you meant. This number does not deliver one, and I am not going to present it as though it does.

At $5,950 a month, the gross rent on a three million dollar asset is roughly 2.4% a year before a single expense is paid. Against private lending at the rates you are accustomed to, that is not a competitive return and I would not argue otherwise.

I could have found a higher number for this report. I could have pointed at the Laureate Park home that leased at $7,550 and left the location difference out of it. You would have felt better reading it, and four months from now the home would still be empty and we would be having a worse conversation than this one.

So here is the honest position, and you are entitled to it before you decide anything.

No rent figure will produce the return you are describing

This is not about my number being conservative. For this home to generate the kind of yield you earn elsewhere, the rent would need to be several times what any renter in this market will pay — and that is true of nearly every luxury home at this value, regardless of who manages it or how it is marketed.

A property at this level is an appreciation and lifestyle asset that happens to be rentable. It is not an income asset, and the rental market will never make it one.

What renting actually does for you

It is worth being precise about the real benefit, because it is not yield.

Renting converts your carrying costs — the property taxes, the insurance, the HOA, the pool and lawn, the upkeep on an empty house — from money leaving your account into money that is largely covered. It also keeps the home occupied, maintained and monitored rather than sitting unoccupied, which carries its own risks and its own insurance implications.

That is a meaningful outcome. It is simply a different one from the return you are measuring against.

And it has a cost you should weigh

A twelve-month lease takes the home off the buyer market for twelve months. If selling remains your priority, that matters more than the rent does, and it is the single most important thing for you to decide before we go any further.

I would rather you make that decision clearly now than discover the tension in month three with a resident in place and a buyer at the door.

If after reading this you decide renting is not the right use of the asset, I will tell you honestly that I think that is a reasonable conclusion, and I would rather hear it now than market a home at a price you never agreed with.

Section Eight

How I would manage it

I will not promise a number of days, and in this segment I would be particularly wrong to try.

What I will do is measure — inquiries, showings booked and showings attended. Because this market moves more slowly, my first checkpoint is day 14 rather than day five — a quiet first week at this price point is normal and not yet information.

If activity by day 14 is not strong enough for me to choose a well-qualified resident rather than accept whoever applies, I move to $5,650. If the market says that is still not enough, I go to $5,395 at day 30 and keep reading it. $5,395 is a step, not a bottom.

I will also watch the Eagle Creek listing closely. It has been sitting 72 days and will have to reduce. When it does, the field moves and I need to move with it rather than three weeks later.

One practical item I would want to settle before launch. Pool and lawn service should remain with you rather than the resident — at this level the cost of a neglected pool far exceeds the service fee, and I do not recommend placing either on a tenant.
